# Budget — money and hours

> Filled by the `define-budget` skill. Two separate budgets that are easy to conflate
> and that fail in different ways: you can afford a credential you have no time to
> prepare for, and you can have all the time in the world for one you cannot pay for.

## Financial

| Item | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ceiling, all-in | *USD 0* | *the number above which the answer is simply no* |
| Comfortable | *USD 0* | *spends without a second thought* |
| Currency | *USD* | |
| Retake budgeted? | *yes / no* | *a failed sitting is a real cost; some exams charge full price again* |
| Who pays | *self / employer / client* | *employer funding often carries its own constraints* |

**All-in** means the exam fee plus anything mandatory to reach it: prerequisite
exams, required courses, membership or partner fees, proctoring, renewal. Headline
exam prices routinely understate this and the research phase must price the whole
route, not the sitting.

## Time

| Item | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours per week available | *0* | *realistic, not aspirational* |
| Weeks available | *0* | *before the window in objectives.md closes* |
| Total study budget | *0 hours* | *hours × weeks* |
| Protected time? | *yes / no* | *a fixed slot, or whatever is left over* |

The total is the number the research phase tests candidates against. A credential
whose honest preparation estimate exceeds it is scored down on time fit even if it
wins on everything else — and the report says so rather than hiding it.
